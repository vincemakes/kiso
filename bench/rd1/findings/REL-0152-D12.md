# REL-0152-D12 — the byte trace, because three rounds of inference failed

- **id:** REL-0152-D12
- **class:** instrument
- **status:** BUILT (`KISO_TRACE_BYTES`, `scripts/trace-brackets.mjs`)

## Why

REL-0152-D1 has now survived three attempts, and every one of them
reasoned from a screenshot. The owner's newest observation is the most
useful yet — the brackets appear when new content is sent and go away
when the session pauses, so they are a property of FRAMES, not of the
settled screen — and it still does not say whether the characters left
kiso or were made by the terminal.

That question has a fact behind it, and the fact is the byte stream.
`[` is the CSI introducer and `]` the OSC introducer, so a lost or
abandoned ESC prints exactly those two characters and nothing else.
kiso emits no OSC at all, and every CSI it writes carries more than its
introducer — so if the bytes leaving kiso are clean, the characters are
made on the other side and the fix is a compatibility one, not a content
one. Nobody can tell those apart by looking at a terminal.

## What it does

`KISO_TRACE_BYTES=<path>` records every byte in BOTH directions with a
millisecond stamp: one JSON object per line, bytes base64 so no escape
or partial UTF-8 sequence is altered on the way to disk. stdout is
wrapped (every path to the terminal goes through it); stdin is TAPPED
with an extra listener, so the editor's own reading is untouched and the
trace cannot swallow a keystroke. Off unless the variable is set.

`scripts/trace-brackets.mjs` reads a capture and reports:

- EDGE brackets — a `[` in a row's first painted column or a `]` in its
  last, which is the reported symptom exactly. Counting brackets proves
  nothing: kiso prints legitimate ones (the faux banner, the extension
  list, the paste capsule), so those are named and set aside.
- writes that END mid-escape, and bare ESC with no sequence. This is the
  mechanism most likely to turn correct bytes into a stray `[`: a
  terminal whose escape parser times out at a chunk boundary abandons
  the sequence and prints the remainder.

## It also answers two other open questions

- **the paste latency.** The editor is measured at 146KB in 6ms with the
  compositor wired, so if a paste still feels slow, the time is being
  spent before kiso sees the bytes. The arrival stamps on the `in`
  records show that directly.
- **REL-0152-D7.** Whether the frame carrying a swallowed message went
  out at all, and what was in it.

## The principle

BM-1 §4, in this project's own words: when evidence is ambiguous, do not
wait for a better world — design the next observation that discriminates
between the competing explanations. Three rounds of looking harder at
the same screenshot is waiting for a better world.
