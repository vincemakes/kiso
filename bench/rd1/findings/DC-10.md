# DC-10 — dim spans render grey-on-grey inside every selection bar

- **id:** DC-10
- **class:** contrast / composition
- **severity:** P1 — the cursor's row in four different pickers
- **agent:** kiso 0.16.3
- **found by:** rendering the ask panel, the model picker, the session
  picker and the `@` picker side by side at width 76 on a light ground
- **status:** FIXED (0.16.4 working tree)

## The measurement

The ask panel's cursor row, SGR intact:

```
\x1b[7m \x1b[1m→ 1   vite\x1b[0m\x1b[7m     \x1b[2mfast dev server…\x1b[0m\x1b[7m … \x1b[27m
```

`\x1b[2m` — dim — **inside** the reverse-video bar. Design §2.1:
*"Nothing dim ever sits on the wash. `#767676` on `#EEEEEE` is 3.91:1
and `#949494` on `#303030` is 4.35:1 — both under the floor."*

The spans that are dim are the option descriptions, the session's
age/turns/note and the `@` picker's directory column: that is, the half
of the row the selection was supposed to help the reader read. The bar
made its own row the least legible one in the list.

## The cause

`selectionBar` exists precisely so there is one copy of this
composition, and its own doc comment says so. Three surfaces had grown
their own copy anyway — `atRow`, `sessionRow`, and the ask panel's
custom row — each rewriting `p.reset` to reset-then-reverse and each
leaving `p.dim` in place, because the rule they were copied from was
about SGR 0 punching a hole in the bar, not about contrast.

## The fix

`selectionBar` drops `p.dim` from the span it wraps — inside the bar and
nowhere else; the same row unselected keeps it, because there the dim IS
the hierarchy. The three hand-rolled copies are deleted and call
`selectionBar`, which is what makes the fix reach all four surfaces at
once rather than the one that was measured.

## Red before green

Two cases in `compositor-kc3-at.test.ts` asserted the dim byte on the
SELECTED row and went red — the assertion moved to an unselected row,
and the selected row now asserts what it must NOT contain
(`\x1b[7m\x1b[2m`, never). The supersession is declared in the file.
