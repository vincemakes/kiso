# REL-0152-D18 — a window drag repainted forty times and left forty copies

- **id:** REL-0152-D18
- **class:** renderer / scrollback pollution
- **severity:** P1 — dragging the window destroys the transcript
- **agent:** kiso 0.15.9
- **found by:** owner dogfood, 2026-08-26
- **status:** FIXED

## The measurement

The owner drags the window edge and the transcript fills with repeated
copies of the same live rows — six or more of one tool block, each
slightly different where a counter had moved on.

SIGWINCH fires continuously during a drag: every few pixels is another
signal. kiso answered each one immediately with an erase and a full
repaint. Gated: forty signals at a drag's cadence produced **forty**
erase-downs.

A real terminal REFLOWS on a width change and pushes the rows it
displaces into its scrollback. So each of those repaints deposited
another copy of the screen into the history, and a two-second drag
deposits dozens.

## Why nothing clever helps afterwards

The scrollback is not ours to rewrite. Once a row is in it, it is in it.
The only fix is to not paint into the middle of a drag.

## The fix

A resize is COALESCED. The geometry is adopted immediately — every cap
and bound is read at frame time, so the model is already at the new size
and nothing is computed against a stale width — and what waits is the
PAINT. When the signals stop for 80ms, one erase and one repaint.

80ms is chosen so a continuous drag never crosses it while a single
deliberate resize still feels immediate; both are gated.

## The structural note worth keeping

The competitor does not have this problem, and the reason is not that it
renders better. It runs on the ALTERNATE SCREEN, which has no scrollback
to accumulate into. kiso is on the primary screen deliberately — the
transcript IS the terminal's own scrollback, which is the product's
whole claim, and what makes a kiso session survive the process that
wrote it.

So this is a cost of a real decision rather than an oversight, and the
right response is to pay it carefully: every repaint kiso makes on the
primary screen is a permanent deposit, and the renderer has to treat
repaints as expensive for that reason and not for a performance one.

## Evidence

`packages/tui/tests/resize-coalesce.test.ts`: forty signals produce at
most one erase-down; the geometry is adopted before the repaint (a live
cap computed at the old height would overflow the new screen); a single
resize still repaints promptly.

Five existing resize cases were updated to let the drag settle — the
repaint's content is unchanged, only its timing moved.
