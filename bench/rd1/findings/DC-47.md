# DC-47 — E3 put prose in the thinking's column, and a piped transcript could no longer tell them apart

- **id:** DC-47
- **class:** §1.2 law violation (a fact that does not survive stripping
  the escapes), introduced by R13's E3
- **severity:** P2 — nothing is lost on a terminal, where italic and dim
  still separate them. Everything is lost in a PIPE, a redirect, a log
  and a paste, which is exactly the surface §1.2 exists for.
- **agent:** R13 (0.24.0), found 2026-09-03 by the PTY reflow gate
  before the round left the machine.
- **status:** FIXED in this round, with a gate on the stripped bytes.
  **The fix is my call, and it is flagged**: the ruling did not
  anticipate the collision, and the owner may prefer to reverse E3
  instead of moving the thinking.

## The measurement

design.md §7.2, unchanged since it was written:

> The indent is the price of §1.2: italic is an escape sequence, so a
> piped transcript would lose the line between the model's reasoning and
> its answer. Two spaces survive as bytes.

R13's E3 moves the model's PROSE to column 2 — the column the thinking
was already in. Rendered through the real components and stripped:

```
thinking: "  Weighing the two shapes."
prose   : "  Weighing the two shapes."
```

Byte-identical. The one carrier §7.2 names was gone.

## How it was caught

Not by reading design.md — by `tui-v4-reflow`, whose discriminator is
`/^ {2}\S/` for "an indented row is thinking" and whose subject is that
no such row has swallowed the response text. With prose at column 2 the
response row matched the thinking pattern and the case failed with
`expected true to be false`.

That is the gate working exactly as intended, and it is the reason it
was written against the INDENT rather than against the italic: a gate
whose discriminator is an escape sequence would have stayed green.

**The general shape, worth keeping:** a round that moves one element
into another element's distinguishing property breaks the property
silently, because nothing about the moved element is wrong. The gate has
to be on the DISTINCTION, not on either side of it.

## The fix, and its alternative

The thinking takes the next column in — column 4 where prose is now at
2. It is still one indent step and still the only carrier that survives
a pipe; what moved is which step, because prose took the one it had.

The alternative is to reverse E3 and leave prose at column 0. That
costs R13's "one left edge" (§1.8), which is a stated goal of the
ruling; the thinking's column is not. So the cheaper thing gives way.

A third option — a glyph on the thinking's rows — is closed by §1.3 and
by §7.2's own argument, which chose an indent over a mark deliberately.

The gate asserts on the STRIPPED bytes at four widths, because that is
the only surface on which the fact it protects exists at all.

## Belongs with

§1.2 (strip every escape and no fact is lost), §7.2 (the thinking's
indent and why it is an indent), §1.8 (one left edge, R13's new law),
and R13's E3.
