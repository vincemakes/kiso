# DC-51 — D1 made every row-per-call group double-space, and the owner saw it as stripes

- **id:** DC-51
- **class:** visual regression, systemic — one rule met five call sites
- **severity:** P2. Nothing is lost and nothing is wrong in the
  transcript; what happens is that groups of rows that belong together
  come out with a blank between every pair, so a card reads as stripes
  and a block is twice the height it should be. On a 24-row terminal
  that difference pushes content off the screen.
- **agent:** the `ctrl+o` expansion since 0.24.2; `/help`, `/rewrap`,
  the trust table and the resume tail since **0.24.0**, when D1 landed.
  Found 2026-09-04 by the owner, from a screenshot of an expanded block.
- **status:** FIXED, red before green.

## The measurement

The owner's screen, expanding a call in 0.24.2: every other row of the
card is the terminal's own white. Reproduced through the real `Body`:

```
per-line:  ["  alpha", "", "  bravo", "", "  charlie"]
one-call:  ["  alpha", "  bravo", "  charlie"]
```

## The mechanism

`bodyLog(text)` is `body.raw(text.split("\n"))` — **one call is one
CELL**. Five places in the CLI printed a group of rows with one call per
row, so the compositor saw N elements rather than one.

D1 (R13, 0.24.0) made the spacing a CONSTANT: exactly one blank between
any two elements, whatever their height. Before it, W11's formula packed
one-row siblings tight, so a row-per-call group happened to render
contiguously — by accident of the formula, not by anyone's intent. D1 is
right and is not what is being reversed here; what it did was reveal
that these five callers had been describing their groups wrongly all
along.

The `ctrl+o` expansion is the one the owner saw because 0.24.2 ③ turned
that block into RENDERED ROWS. Before, its lines were a header and two
multi-line payloads — four cells, with the payloads' own newlines
keeping their rows inside one cell each, so only four blanks appeared
and they read as section spacing.

## The five

| where | what it prints |
|---|---|
| `dispatch.ts` ctrl+o | the expanded card |
| `dispatch.ts` `/rewrap` | the re-wrapped prose |
| `dispatch.ts` `/help` | the keys and commands |
| `trust-ui.ts` | the project trust table |
| `index.ts` ×3 | the resume tail |

All five now make ONE call. `bodyLog` splits on newlines and a rendered
row may not contain one (invariant ①b throws on it), so joining is
exactly reversible.

## A correction to DC-50

DC-50 recorded that an expansion's uncapped body "pushes its own head
row off a 24-row screen". That was measured, but the cause was this
defect: the striping made the block more than twice as tall as the card.
With the rows contiguous the head row is on screen, and the gate now
asserts exactly that. DC-50's remaining content — that `ctrl+o` appends
because §7.1 forbids redrawing, and that route B's reprint is what
changes it — stands.

## Why no gate caught it

Every gate on the expansion asserted CONTENT and ORDER — which rows are
there, and in what sequence. Interleaved blank rows disturb neither. The
new gate asserts the thing the owner could see: press the key, and the
row naming what you opened is ON THE SCREEN, with no unpainted blanks
laced through the card.

**The general shape, worth keeping:** a change to a spacing RULE does
not announce which callers were relying on the old one. D1 was gated on
its own behaviour — one blank between two elements — and every one of
those gates passed. What none of them asked was whether the things being
spaced were the elements their authors meant.

## Belongs with

D1 (R13), 0.24.2 ③ (the expansion as a card), DC-50, and §1.7's rhythm.
