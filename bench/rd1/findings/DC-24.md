# DC-24 — the fold does not fire on the turns that most need it

- **id:** DC-24
- **class:** product shape / the R3 round's own subject
- **severity:** P2 — the collapse the owner asked for did not happen in
  the first real session after shipping it
- **agent:** kiso 0.16.7
- **found by:** the 0.16.7 real-model dogfood
- **status:** OPEN — the rule is right; whether the COST is acceptable
  is the owner's call

## The observation

A real read-only session: 28 tool calls, 20 model round-trips, 110s.
Fold lines emitted: **zero.** The settled scrollback carried 23 body
rows and then the cost line.

The turn hit both blockers at once:

- **trouble.** 2 of the 28 calls failed (DC-23). `#segmentHasTrouble`
  keeps every row of a turn that hit any failure — law 1.3, and the
  code comment states the cost outright: "a turn that reads twenty
  files and hits one denial keeps all twenty rows."
- **spill.** 23 rows against a 30-row live region (H=34), before the
  streaming answer. R3f forbids a fold that would claim rows already in
  the scrollback.

Either one alone is sufficient.

## Why it matters

The cost was accepted when it was assumed rare. This session says it is
not: an ordinary read-only exploration hit a 7% tool failure rate, and
any single failure is enough. The fold fires on small, clean turns —
which are the turns whose rows were not a problem in the first place.

The owner's ask was "each tool runs on its own row, and when the work
is over the whole of it becomes one line". On this session it never
became one line.

## What is NOT wrong

Both rules are correct in themselves:

- summarising a turn that failed, as though it had not, is the
  truthfulness defect law 1.3 exists to prevent;
- a fold cannot retract rows the terminal has already scrolled away.

The rollups DID work — `explored`, ` · 112 lines · ctrl+r` — so the
turn was 23 rows rather than 28.

## The question for the owner

Three shapes, none of them free:

1. **Trouble narrows to the CELL, not the turn.** The failed call keeps
   its row with its words; the rest of the turn folds, and the fold
   says so: `✦ thought 110s · read 12 files · ran 12 searches · 2
   failed · ctrl+r`. Nothing is summarised away — the failure is named
   ON the fold line and its own row survives.
2. **Fix the failure rate instead** (DC-23 and its kin), and leave the
   rule alone. Cheaper, and it does nothing for the spill blocker.
3. **Accept it**: the fold is for short turns, and long turns are read
   by scrolling.

(1) is the one that serves the ask without weakening law 1.3, and it is
a round of its own: it changes what `#turnHasTrouble` gates and adds a
term to the fold's vocabulary.
