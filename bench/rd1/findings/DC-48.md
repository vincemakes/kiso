# DC-48 — the three-row card's one row was assembled outside the cutting discipline, and it killed the session

- **id:** DC-48
- **class:** invariant ① violation, reached on the first frame of an
  ordinary command
- **severity:** **P1** — kiso THROWS and the session ends. The human
  loses the composer's contents and the run. Reached by any command
  whose text plus its status exceeds the terminal's width, which on an
  80-column terminal is an everyday `find` or `grep`.
- **agent:** 0.24.0. Found 2026-09-04 in the owner's dogfood, first
  frame of the first command.
- **status:** FIXED in 0.24.1, red before green.

## The measurement

80 columns, `shell find ~ -maxdepth 3 -type d \( -iname '*kiso*' \)
2>/dev/null | grep -v node_modules | head -40`:

```
kiso-tui invariant ① violated: a line of visible width 113 > 80 was
about to be emitted
  at #checked ← #drawFull ← Body.render
```

Reproduced off the compositor at 80, 60 and 40 columns: 113, 93, 73.

## The mechanism

R13's running branch cut its head row to `W` with `gutterCut`, then
handed it to `slabBlock`. `slabBlock`'s no-body branch joins head and
outcome into ONE row — `${head} ${outcome}` — and cut nothing. So the
row came out `W` wide PLUS the entire status row.

The state it happens in is a running call with nothing back yet, which
after DC-46 is the three-row card — **the first second of every
command**.

The settled three-row card was never exposed: `settledHeadText` builds
its own chain against the room it has. Only the live form crashed.

**Same shape as DC-45**: a row assembled AFTER the fold, by a caller who
did not think of itself as making a row. That is twice in two days.

## The fix

1. The running branch assembles the three-row card's single row itself,
   against the room it actually has, in pin 4's order: the command is
   the cuttable span, the elapsed is never cut open, and the shell's
   gestures give way before either.
2. `slabBlock`'s no-body branch cuts the joined row to `W` — the
   backstop that makes invariant ① hold whatever a caller does.
3. A second defect the gate found while being written: at very narrow
   widths `settledHeadText`'s last tier returned the target ALONE,
   dropping the elapsed. Its stated reason was "no half-open
   parenthesis", and R13's `·` chain has no bracket to leave open, so
   the reason retired with the parentheses. Pin 4 now holds at every
   width.
4. The `·` the head row was missing between target and elapsed
   (`● shell npm test 1s` → `● shell npm test · 1s`).

## The gate

DC-45's lesson, applied: **the fixture does not sit at a boundary.** One
command long enough to overflow every width, walked from W=20 to W=200,
on a painted and an unpainted ground, running and settled — every row
asserted ≤ W and the elapsed asserted present.

## What else this round changed, because of it

Invariant ① is now a CRASH UNDER TEST and a CUT IN THE FIELD (see
design.md §1.2's neighbourhood and the components header). Two
instances of this class in two days, one caught by a gate and one by the
owner, is the evidence that "the crash is the contract" is right in a
suite and wrong in a human's hands.

is reached from.
