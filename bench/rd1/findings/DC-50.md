# DC-50 — ctrl+o appends because it cannot redraw, and route B is what changes that

- **id:** DC-50
- **class:** design consequence of §7.1, not a defect
- **severity:** P3 — nothing is wrong and nothing is lost. What it costs
  is that the expansion lands somewhere the reader did not press, and
  cannot be put back.
- **agent:** every version. Named 2026-09-04 after the owner read an
  appended block that had landed under the turn recap.
- **status:** OPEN — the solution is route B's, and route B is 0.25.0.
  0.24.2 fixed the SHAPE (the block is a card now, and `✦` is gone);
  this finding is the part that shape cannot fix.

## The constraint

§7.1: a row that has entered the terminal's scrollback is never
re-emitted, reflowed or erased (ADR-0046). So kiso cannot expand a
committed call IN PLACE — there is no way to make the rows below it move
down. It appends instead, at the bottom, wherever the bottom happens to
be.

On a turn that has ended, the bottom is after the recap. So the reader
presses a key on one thing and a block appears somewhere else, with only
its own head row to say what it belongs to. 0.24.2 makes that head row a
card's — the call named, the target bold — which is as far as shape can
carry it.

**And the block pushes its own head off.** Its body is uncapped — an
expansion that capped would be no expansion — so a long one on a
24-row terminal scrolls past its own head row, which is the row that
names what was opened. Appending an uncapped block cannot avoid that;
only the reprint can, by putting the expansion where the call is.

## What the reference implementation does, and why it can

Its `ctrl+o` is not aimed at a cell at all: it is a GLOBAL toggle
(`toolOutputExpanded`) that applies to every tool block at once, and the
frame that follows repaints the WHOLE screen. It can do that because
repainting the screen is what it does anyway — the same mechanism kiso's
§7.1 forbids.

That is a real difference in kind, not a feature kiso is missing: kiso's
committed rows are the terminal's, and the terminal's scrollback is the
transcript. The price of that is this.

## The route B solution

Route B (R11 Part 2, 0.25.0) brings the reprint: on a settled resize,
`2J H 3J` and print the session again at the new width. Once the product
has a mechanism for re-printing what it has already said, `ctrl+o` can
take the same shape as the reference's —

1. flip a global `expanded` flag;
2. reprint.

— and expansion becomes in-place for every block at once, which is also
the answer to "which one did I just open": all of them.

**Not implemented here, and not to be implemented before route B**: a
reprint built for `ctrl+o` alone would be route B's mechanism arriving
through the back door, without route B's own gates (DC-40's cursor
homing, the scrollback duplication bounds, the resize family's
measurements). One mechanism, one round.

## Belongs with

§7.1 and ADR-0046 (committed rows are final), R11 Part 2 / route B
(0.25.0), §9's transcript viewer — which exists precisely because
expand-in-place is impossible — and 0.24.2 ③, which fixed everything
about this that shape could.
