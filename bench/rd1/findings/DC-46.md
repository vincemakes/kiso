# DC-46 — with the activity slot retired, the live region and the window's top cannot both be free

- **id:** DC-46
- **class:** design consequence of R13, measured in four states
- **severity:** the FIRST fix was P1 in practice (see below); the state
  the tree is in now costs a transient blank band above the composer.
- **agent:** R13 (0.24.0). Found 2026-09-03 while re-deriving R7a's
  standing-rows gates; the first fix's defect found by fable's review the
  same day.
- **status:** **MEASURED, AWAITING ADJUDICATION.** fable pre-registered
  a stop rule — *if hole-frames exceed 10% of the a7 session, stop and
  report the numbers before closing* — and it is tripped. The tree
  carries the only state of the four that neither loses rows nor loses
  the running call's output; it is not marked FIXED.

## The four states, measured on the a7 replay

Hole-frames = frames whose longest interior blank run exceeds 2 rows,
over the 733-frame reviewer-dogfood replay, at three geometries. The a7
A8 guard (`maxBlankRun <= 2` after the screen durably fills) passes in
every state; this is the frequency, not the guard.

| state | 40x24 | 80x24 | 40x12 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.23.0**, before this round | 8.9% | 13.5% | 3.8% | the baseline — and already over 10% at 80x24 |
| R13 + `#liveRoom` | 8.9% | 13.5% | 3.5% | **loses the running call's output** on any session past one screen |
| R13, screen-bounded, floor scroll | 12.0% | 16.6% | 5.2% | **LOSES ROWS OUTRIGHT** — see below |
| R13, screen-bounded, `skip` scroll — **the tree** | 16.9% | 24.6% | 7.9% | neither loss; this is the cost |

**The baseline matters.** The hole is not R13's invention: 0.23.0 runs
at 8.9 / 13.5 / 3.8, and 80x24 was already past the 10% line before this
round began. What R13 adds is +8.0 / +11.1 / +4.1 points.

## What R4 was buying, and with what
## What R4 was buying, and with what

R4's standing activity slot held the live region at a CONSTANT height
for a whole stretch: a call starting or finishing swapped the slot's
contents and never its size. That is what made R7a A true — *a row that
is on the screen does not move DOWN* — because the window's top is read
off the total content height, and a total that never changes cannot
move the top.

R13 retires the slot: every call is its own card, and a card's height
depends on what the call did. The live region therefore breathes, and
the property has to come from somewhere else.

## The first fix, and why it was wrong

`#liveRoom` capped the live region at `H - chrome - #committedLines`, so
that `skip` — the window's top — depended only on `#committedLines`,
which only grows. The argument was right about `skip` and **wrong about
the quantity**: `#committedLines` is CUMULATIVE. It is re-derived over
the whole line cache every frame and counts rows that left for the
terminal's scrollback long ago. One screenful into any session it
exceeds `H`, the room clamps to its floor of one row, and every running
call after that is a head row with its output gone.

Measured at 24 AND 40 rows, with eight blank rows still on the screen:

```
● shell npm test · 1s · ctrl+o
────────────────────────────────────────────
```

That is not "a short terminal with a lot of committed work" — it is
every session that runs longer than one screen. Found by fable's review,
with a probe (`capture-liveroom.ts`) that is now the red case in
`r13-one-surface`.

## And the second, which lost rows

Removing the cap alone was not enough. `#emitScroll`'s target was a
FLOOR — where the window's top would sit with the live band empty —
chosen conservatively because the top could come back down when the live
region shrank, and a scroll is the frame's one irreversible act.

With a live region that can now grow by a whole card, `skip` runs well
past that floor, and the rows between them were **painted over in place,
never scrolled: present in neither the screen nor the scrollback.**
Measured on R7a D — a four-call burst on a 24-row terminal, and the
first call's head row (`package-lock.json`) was never emitted at all,
`scrollback.length === 0`.

That is the `lost` class the scrollback instruments exist to name, and
it is strictly worse than any hole. The target is `skip` itself now: the
top is monotone (the clamp), so a row above it has left for good and
there is nothing conservative left to be.

## Where it comes from now

`#liveRoom` caps the live region at what the committed rows leave:

```
liveRoom = max(1, H - CHROME_ROWS - inputExtra - queueRows - committedLines)
```

With the live region inside that budget, `skip` — the window's top —
depends only on `#committedLines`, which only ever grows. So the top
never falls, and R7a A holds. Measured across the sweep (60x16, 74x16,
100x24, 132x40, one- and two-row thoughts): **no row moves down at all
at seven of the eight, and by exactly one row at 100x24**, at the frame
where a four-call burst is half settled. Every offender across the sweep
was +1; none was larger. R7a A2 already prices a one-row shift at the
turn boundary, so the bound is the one the file already lives with.

## The cost, measured

The floor is one row. On a terminal where the committed rows already
fill the screen, the live region gets that one row, and DC-43's shrink
takes the running card down to its head:

```
74x24, four reads committed (16 rows) and a shell running:

  read  package-lock.json          read  .github/workflows/ci.yml
  └ x                              └ x
    1 line · 0.0s                    1 line · 0.0s
  …                                …
  ● shell npm run check · 1s · ctrl+o      ← its live output is gone
```

Under R4 the four reads folded into ONE line and the shell had the rest
of the screen. The `114 passed` its output was showing is now behind the
key. R7a D's subject — *a call still running is never the one truncated
away* — is the rule this violates, and it is older and, in the owner's
own words at the time, about the thing the human is actually waiting for.

## The other option, and ITS cost

Give the live region the room it needs and let the committed rows scroll
off — which is what a scrollback is for — then keep the window's top
from coming back by clamping it monotone within the turn.

That was BUILT AND MEASURED under R7a, before R13, and rejected: a
monotone top holds the window below the content, and the a7 replay's
blank-run guard goes from **65 to 692 of 733 frames** at 40x24 — a
three-row hole above the composer through most of a real session. The
measurement is in R7a's own source comment and has not been re-taken
under R13, where the live region shrinks back as calls finish and the
hole may close differently.

## What the current state costs, stated plainly

A settle gives rows back; the window's top is clamped and cannot follow;
so a transient blank band appears above the composer, bounded by the
shrink itself (a twelve-row card becoming a three-row one, plus D1's
blank — **ten rows**, and R7a A gates exactly that bound, measured at 10
once at 100x24 and 0-2 everywhere else). The next commit fills it.

The frequency is the table at the top: 16.9 / 24.6 / 7.9 percent of
frames, against a 0.23.0 baseline of 8.9 / 13.5 / 3.8.

## The choice

1. **As it stands.** The running call always has its window; a settle
   leaves a transient band above the composer, bounded at ten rows and
   filled by the next commit. 16.9 / 24.6 / 7.9 percent of frames.
2. **Accept a smaller card while a call runs** — cap the running
   window below the settled one, so the shrink at settle is smaller.
   Costs E2 as written ("allocated at its settled height") and buys a
   proportionally smaller band. Not built, not costed.
3. **Anchor the live region under the committed content rather than to
   the composer** when the content fills the window, so the band sits
   below the live region instead of above it. Same row count, different
   place; whether that reads better is a taste question and belongs with
   a screenshot, not a percentage.

Not decided here, and the stop rule is why: the number is over the line
that was set before the measurement was taken. The gates record the
current behaviour with its bound derived (R7a A's ten rows, R7a D on the
scrollback), so a change of ruling is a change of two assertions and not
an archaeology exercise.

## Belongs with

R4 (the standing slot), R7a A/A2/D (the standing-rows gates and the
monotone-skip measurement), DC-43 (the shrink), DC-33 and TUI2-R2pre ②
(a window top that falls is a window that un-scrolls), and R13's ruling,
