# DC-34 — the widen seam. OPEN.

**Status**: FIXED, adjudicated. Two attempts failed first and are kept
below: each was wrong in a different way, and the pair is what located
the real question.
**Reported by**: the owner, twice, from live sessions
**Round**: R8b, 2026-09-01

## What the owner sees

Widening the terminal during a turn: prose appears twice — once folded
narrow and cut mid-sentence, once folded wide and complete.

## The mechanism

Every count in `packages/tui/src/compositor.ts` is physical ROWS at the
fold width in force when it was computed: `#committedLines`,
`#lineCache`, `skip`, `floor`, `#scrolledOff`. A resize sets
`#fullRedraw`, which refolds every committed cell at the new W and
recomputes `#committedLines`. On a widen the same text takes FEWER rows,
so every index into `all` shifts — and the resize frame then adopts
`#scrolledOff = max(#scrolledOff, min(skip, all.length))`, keeping a
count of NARROW rows as an index into WIDE ones. Nothing translates it,
and nothing can: the row the scroll stopped at does not exist in the new
fold.

Two consequences, and they are the same off-by-a-refold:

- the window paints from text the terminal already holds — the duplicate
  band;
- afterwards `leaving <= 0` suppresses emission until `#committedLines`
  regrows past the stale count, and the text that marches past in
  between never enters the scrollback — the hole.

## What the gates measure, and the timing that decides which defect appears

`apps/cli/tests/dc34-widen-seam.test.ts`. The discriminator the owner's
screenshots could never be: the faux script IS the model, and every
~30-column span carries a serial token written EXACTLY ONCE — asserted
by the first case. So a token on two lines is the renderer duplicating,
and a token on no line is the renderer losing. "The model repeated
itself" is impossible by construction.

The timing matters more than anything else here, and getting it wrong
cost this round its first fix:

| the winch lands... | duplicate | hole |
|---|---|---|
| after streaming stops (quiet composer, content scrolled) | YES | no |
| mid-answer, deltas still flowing (`delay` events pacing the script) | no | **YES** |

An unpaced faux script plays every event in one burst, so a driver that
waits for a marker in the pty stream fires its winch after the turn is
already over. The first version of this file did exactly that, and its
green measured a quiet-composer resize while claiming the mid-answer
one. The `delay` pseudo-event (W18, `packages/evals/src/faux.ts`) is
what makes the scenario real; the script uses it now.

Two cases are `it.fails` — asserted to FAIL — because the defect is
open. They run on the real binary every time, and the day a fix lands
they invert and force someone back here.

## The fix — three rules

The record of what reached the terminal already existed: `#scrolledOff`.
Rows below it are in the terminal's scrollback, at the width they left
at, and no path of ours may contradict them. What was missing was every
path honouring it.

1. **The refold is frontier-scoped.** A committed cell is refolded iff
   it is ENTIRELY ABOVE the frontier, or one of its cached rows is wider
   than W. The second disjunct is not a weakening of the first: a cell
   can STRADDLE the frontier — head inked, tail still on screen — and
   the tail must be painted at the current width. Holding its commit
   fold there put a 100-column row into an 80-column screen and
   invariant ① threw (60 → 100 → 80, measured). Fitting wins; a crash
   is worse than a seam, and the seam a narrowing leaves is rider 2's.
2. **The adopt runs only when a refold actually ran.** With no refold
   there are no indices to translate, so `#scrolledOff` must not move.
3. **The march never reaches below the frontier**, on a frame whose
   WIDTH changed. Without it the unpaced widen duplicates six tokens,
   stably, three runs of three. Clamping on every frame instead breaks
   the A8 windowing case — DC-37 records why that is the same root.

### The two attempts that failed first

- **the last-refold width.** Crashed on 60 → 100 → 80: after a widen the
  cache is MIXED, and a cell committed at 100 was emitted into an
  80-column screen. Taught that the predicate must ask about the CACHE,
  not about the last render.
- **the cache's widest fold.** Fires a full refold at the first
  narrowing, re-wrapping rows the scrollback already holds — the
  original defect, alive in the other direction. Taught that no scalar
  expresses the question; the frontier is what both were approximating.

A latch bug hid inside the second and cost this round a wrong
conclusion: `#refolded` was armed by the session's FIRST frame (a
vacuous refold over zero committed cells) and never reset, so every
session's first widen still ran the adopt and swallowed the live band's
worth of committed rows — three paragraphs, in the measurement that
found it. It resets at the top of every frame now.

### Measured

- paced AND unpaced × 60→100, 40→120, 60→100→110: duplicated 0,
  missing 0. The pacing matters — see the timing table above.
- 60→100→80, 60→100→50, 100→60, 40→120→70, 60→100→80→120: no
  invariant ①.
- DC-33's own scenario, judged by CONTENT: duplicated 0, missing 0.

### The two gates the adjudication re-scoped

- **DC-33's LF count was a proxy, and HEAD passed it BECAUSE of the
  hole.** The stale adopt suppressed every post-winch commit: LF=0 here
  and prose never reaching history there — the gate and the defect were
  one code path. Split into the literal invariant (the winch's own
  repaint frame emits no LF of ours) and the protection (no row
  committed before the winch stands on two history lines).
- **v6 ② asserted path independence, which the ink contract forbids.**
  A storm through a geometry short enough to scroll committed rows away
  leaves them in the scrollback at the width they left at; the direct
  path never put them there. Equalising the two means reaching into the
  terminal's scrollback — what ADR-0046 refuses. Restated over the
  family where equality is achievable: storms whose intermediates never
  overflow. The condition is NOT "no width narrower than the
  destination" — that reading was proposed and falsified on both edges,
  by a storm dipping to 20 columns that stays identical (tall heights,
  the frontier never moves) and one never below 40 that diverges (a
  20-ROW intermediate overflows). The seam is asserted from the other
  side too, so the exception cannot quietly widen.

## What ADR-0046 already sanctioned, and what it did not

Read late in the round, and it reframes the defect. ADR-0046's
trade-off note says, of the resize:

> the resize's first frame RE-PAINTS the committed content (the
> screen-state == frame-state rule — the visible screen is the
> invariant), so a shrink storm re-emits the committed lines into the
> NATIVE scrollback — at most one extra copy per resize, strictly
> bounded. The scrollback is explicitly NOT the invariant; this is the
> accepted cost of the rule.

So **one bounded extra copy per resize was already accepted**. What the
owner hit was not that:

- **the hole** — prose vanishing from history entirely. Sanctioned
  nowhere, and the reason DC-33's gate was green on HEAD.
- **the same prose at TWO fold widths, the first cut mid-sentence** —
  not "an extra copy", which is what the note bounds. It is a copy of a
  DIFFERENT rendering, which is what an untranslated index across a
  refold produces.

This round's rules narrow what the note permits: the frame no longer
repaints committed rows on a widen at all. That is a tightening of a
sanctioned cost, in the direction the owner asked for, and it is why
v6 ② had to be re-scoped rather than left alone — see above.

## The reflow question this leaves open

The other implementation DOES reflow its whole visible transcript on a
resize. Measured, not assumed: it emits no `3J`, no alternate screen,
and almost no absolute addressing — it REPRINTS, pushing the old copy
up and writing a re-wrapped one below (42 LFs after a widen, starting
again from its banner). Its history is its own buffer, so reprinting
costs it nothing.

kiso cannot copy that as-is: our history IS the user's native
scrollback (ADR-0040 route B), so a reprint puts a second copy in their
shell history — permanently, once per resize.

The version worth a round of its own: reprint only what is still on the
PHYSICAL screen — move the ink line from "committed" to "scrolled off",
which is exactly `#scrolledOff`, the record this round made trustworthy.
Everything visible reflows, the scrollback never doubles, and the cost
is a seam at the screen boundary (the straddling cell, which this round
already had to solve for). It needs V6-1 restated and is not a fix that
may ride a defect round.

**Ruled, 2026-09-02.** Measured on Apple Terminal with the installed
0.20.3 (`kiso-doc/tui-recon-2026-09-02/e2e-terminal-app/`): the widen
above is clean; a NARROWING leaves a four-token duplicate band (rider
2, seen); a height GROW destroys as many rows of history as it added
(DC-39, P1). The owner chose the other implementation's route — erase
the terminal and reprint the session at the new width — over the
frontier design, after both were measured side by side. The plan is
R11 (kiso-doc); DC-40 lands first. This section's open question is
closed by that ruling.

## Rider 3 — every path that repaints committed rows

| site | what it is | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `enter()` | the session's first frame | safe — nothing committed above it |
| `#settleResize` | the resize's one repaint | **the defect** |
| `onGroundChange` | rides `#settleResize` for DC-3's recolor | **in scope** — same trigger, different duty |
| `#emitScroll` staging | repaints rows 1..chunk before the LFs | must stay enumerated: it writes into the SCROLLBACK, where a wrong byte is permanent |
| the overlay close | repaints displaced rows from `#lastSkip` | sanctioned, but it is such a path |
| the guard-wipe class | any external write forgets `#screen`; the next frame repaints every committed row | the design pillar option C strictly read collides with |
| `expandNext` | ctrl+r | already append-only |
| `/rewrap` | re-prints recent prose at the current width | the sanctioned ADR-0046 answer to a stale fold |

That last row of the middle block is the escalation this finding leaves
for the owner: the diff renderer's self-heal contract means "committed
rows are never repainted" conflicts with a pillar older than this round.
Option C strictly read needs that reconciled — it is not something a
round may settle by editing its own gate.
