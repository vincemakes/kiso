# DC-32 — the pad was covering a hole

**Status**: FIXED (R7a)
**Reported by**: the owner, from screenshots of a live session
**Round**: R7a, 2026-08-31

## What the owner saw

Four reports across one session, which turned out to be one defect
seen from four sides:

1. a blank line appeared between the thought and the call at the
   settle (reported as: it was flush before, and after it finished
   there was a gap);
2. every row of a parallel burst wore its own breathing mark, and on a
   read that returns in 200ms the mark was gone before the eye landed
   (reported as: there is no need for one in front of every read row);
3. a long vertical bar ran down the screen under short blocks, and
   vanished when the stream ended;
4. the names of finished calls disappeared one at a time, so a
   four-file burst ended having read four files and shown none of them.

## The defect

The act slot (R4) held a fixed height by PADDING itself to a budget,
and drew the pad as `│ ` — the body gutter. That single decision
produced all four:

- the pad was a gutter on rows with nothing on them (law 1.3), which
  is (3);
- padding to a FIXED budget meant the block reserved rows it had
  nothing to put in — after R7 moved thinking out of the slot, the
  pre-tool phase of every stretch reserved its whole budget for empty;
- the pad existed because the slot's content came and went: a finished
  call LEFT the block, which is (4);
- the block, being multi-row, took a W11 blank its one-row committed
  fold did not, so the blank appeared or vanished at the settle
  depending on the thought's height, which is (1);
- the mark rode each call because the activity had no row of its own
  to wear it, which is (2).

Blanking the gutter (the first fix) revealed the hole it had been
covering: the a7 dogfood replay's blank-run guard went from 65 to 653
of 733 frames at 40x24 — the screen never durably filling.

## The fix

The pad goes. The height it was buying is bought by the CONTENT: every
call of a stretch keeps its row for the life of the stretch, so a
block whose rows only accumulate cannot shrink, and nothing above it
can be pulled down. In flight rows are admitted first and are never the
ones truncated; the finished names fill what is left, newest first; the
activity line carries the one breathing mark, lit while and only while
a call is actually running. The block takes the spacing its FOLD will
take, so the settle changes a row's content and never its position.

## What was tried and rejected, measured

- **A monotone `skip`** (the window never scrolls back). It removes the
  turn boundary's one-row shift exactly, and holds the window BELOW the
  content: a7's blank-run frame goes 65 -> 692 of 733. One row of
  motion once per turn, as the answer lands, is cheaper than a
  three-row hole above the composer through most of a session. The
  boundary's shift is now BOUNDED at one row by its own gate.
- **A per-stretch slot high-water** (pad only to what this stretch has
  used). It did not make the region monotone — the re-composition is
  the stretch line and the spacing, not the slot alone — and it put
  a7's hole back. Rejected on both counts.

## Gates

`packages/tui/tests/r7a-standing-rows.test.ts` — 25 gates measuring the
SUBJECT (where rows sit, frame over frame) rather than R4's height
proxy, at four widths. Six of the twenty-five are red on the
pre-ruling tree; the rest are regression gates, and the file says which
are which.

R4's own proxy assertions are rewritten in place with the supersession
recorded at each site. R4 C's clamp setup was re-tuned: with the pad
gone, one running shell fits inside H=8 on its own, so the old setup
had stopped exercising the clamp at all — it now uses a burst, and
asserts that the tall form really does exceed the short screen before
comparing them.
