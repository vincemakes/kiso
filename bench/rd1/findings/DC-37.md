# DC-37 — `#scrolledOff` conflates two kinds of scrolled-away row

**Status**: OPEN, recorded rather than fixed
**Found by**: the DC-34 adjudication, 2026-09-01
**Round**: R8b coda

## The conflation

`#scrolledOff` counts rows that are no longer on screen. Two very
different things land in that one number:

- **rows a line feed pushed away.** They are in the terminal's
  scrollback at the width they left at, and nothing brings them back.
- **rows a resize ADOPTED.** The window shrank, the terminal reflowed
  or the model re-indexed, and the count moved without any of ours
  being emitted. Grow the terminal again and those rows come back on
  screen — the A8 windowing case, which has a gate.

The march must reach back into the second and never into the first.
`#scrolledOff` cannot tell them apart, so DC-34's rule 3 keys its
clamp on a proxy instead: clamp on a frame whose WIDTH changed, leave
a height change alone.

## Why the proxy holds, and where it does not

It holds because a height change re-indexes nothing — re-rendering at
the same width is deterministic, so every row means what it meant.
That is a property of the code, not of the scenario, and it is what
keeps the A8 case green (verified: unit 1972/1972).

It is still a proxy. The clean separation would be a per-row record of
how a row left. The place this is most likely to surface next is a
HEIGHT change after a NARROWING on a terminal that does not reflow:
the narrowing's rows left at the old width, and a later grow brings
back rows the model believes it may repaint.

Clamping unconditionally is not the answer and was measured: it breaks
the A8 case, which is the same conflation biting from the other side.

## Why it is recorded and not fixed

DC-34's three rules fix a defect the owner hit twice, on their exact
exposure, with no collateral. This is the next layer down, it has no
reproduction yet, and inventing one belongs to the round that fixes
it. Written down so that round does not have to re-derive the reason.
